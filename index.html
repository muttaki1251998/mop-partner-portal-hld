Partner Portal & CRM

High-level design · v1 · for review

A private portal for our trade partners, run from one internal CRM.

Today the “partner login” in the footer goes nowhere. This plan gives approved clients a real account where they see their pricing — and gives our team one place to invite clients, group them, and set the discounts they see.

02

How pricing works

The heart of the system. A client’s price is built in three layers. Each layer can adjust the one above it — and the most specific rule always wins.

Worked example · one sofa, one Gold-tier client

1

Base price · from Contentful

The list price of the product. Same for everyone until a discount applies.

$4,000
2

Tier discount · the client’s group

This client is in the Gold tier — 15% off everything.

$4,000$3,400
3

Per-product discount · overrides the tier

This sofa has its own 25% deal. It replaces the tier discount for this item.

$4,000$3,000
◆
Rule of thumb: the most specific discount wins  —  Per-product › Tier › Base. A product-level deal isn’t added on top of the tier; it takes its place for that one product.
Option A

Discount a whole tier

Set one percentage for a group of clients — “Gold gets 15% off.” Fast, and it applies across the catalogue. Good for relationship-based pricing.

Option B

Discount a single product

Put a deal on one product that overrides whatever tier discount would apply. Good for clearing stock or a one-off promotion.

Where per-product pricing lives

A dedicated Product Pricing section

The CRM has its own screen that lists every product published in Contentful — pulled in automatically, so the list is always current. Next to each product we can set an individual discount. Set one and it becomes that product’s per-product deal, overriding the tier discount for everyone. Leave it blank and the product simply follows each client’s tier. It’s the one place to see, at a glance, which products have their own pricing and which don’t.

03

Showing the discount is a choice

By default the client just sees their (already lower) price — no “30% off” banner. When we want to make a deal feel like a deal, an admin flips a switch and the savings become visible.

Show discount · OFF (default)
Aria Lounge Sofa
$3,000

The client sees only their price. Clean, quiet, no comparison — the number simply is what it is.

Show discount · ON
Aria Lounge Sofa
$4,000$3,000
Save 25%

Same price — now the original and the saving are shown, so the value is obvious.

04

Inviting a client

Clients never sign themselves up. We invite them by email from the CRM — that’s the only way in, so every account is one we approved.

1

Admin sends invite

Enter the client’s email in the CRM and hit invite.

2

Client gets email

A secure link lands in their inbox. Status shows Pending.

3

They set a password

The link lets them create their own password — we never see it.

4

Account is active

They can log in and see their pricing. Status shows Active.

Forgot password

A client can reset their own password anytime by email — no need to call us. Same secure-link approach as the invite.

What admins can see

  • Whether an invite is still pending or the account is active
  • When each client last logged in
  • Which tier they’re in and every inquiry they’ve sent

◇

Decisions we’ll want from the team

Nothing blocking — just the choices that shape the first version.

Q1

What are our tiers? Names and default discounts — e.g. Standard (0%), Silver (10%), Gold (15%). We can start with a few and add more later.

Q2

Who counts as an admin? You’re the first. Which teammates should also be able to invite clients and set discounts?

Q3

Should the “show discount” switch be global or per-tier? One switch for the whole site, or the ability to reveal savings to some tiers but not others.
